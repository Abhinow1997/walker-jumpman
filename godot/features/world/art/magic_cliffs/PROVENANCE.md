## The music

The pack ships one track beside the sheets, at
`Assets/magic cliffs music/magic cliffs.ogg`: a 96-second stereo loop, 48 kHz,
192 kbps nominal. `scripts/extract_magic_cliffs.py` copies it to
`godot/audio/magic_cliffs.ogg` — copied rather than processed, since there is
nothing to cut out of an ogg — and `godot/game/music.gd` loops it.

Everything that names a track names this one, because the pack has only one:
the title screen and all four course levels. That is why a new journey does not
restart it, and why PRACTICE carries it on. The greybox fixture names no track
and is silent. A boss fight is the exception — The Fractured Isles and The
Dragon's Roost name `decisive_battle` as `boss_music` and swap to it while the
boss is alive; that track is not from this pack, see `godot/audio/PROVENANCE.md`.

**Its licence rests on the same evidence as the art's**, and no more: the music
came inside the CC0 download from the OpenGameArt listing, and that listing is
what was read on 2026-09-14. The file carries no licence of its own and no
separate verification was made for it. If the project ever needs a rights audit
rather than a coursework note, the music should be re-checked against the
listing alongside the sheets — it is the same download, so the same answer is
expected, but it has not been confirmed independently.

The track is 48 kHz and so is Godot's mixer here, so nothing is resampled on the
way out.

Same author as `Assests/Streets of Fight files/`, which is also CC0 and carries
its own `public-license.pdf`.

## Why this matters here

Every other sheet in this project is either ripped commercial art or generated:
the Anti-Davis player is a Little Fighter 2 fan mod, the Bandit and the items are
LF2 rips, and the title screen and the HUD bars were generated. That is fine for
coursework and would be a takedown on itch.io or Steam, both of which require you
to warrant you hold the rights. (The unattributable `mech-healthbar.png` that
used to be named here is no longer used — see `ui/art/PROVENANCE.md`.)

This pack is the first art in the game that carries no such problem. Terrain,
background and set dressing for The Fractured Isles are all CC0.

## What is used

Generated by `scripts/extract_magic_cliffs.py` from `Assets/Environment/PNG/`.
**Never hand-edit anything in this folder** — rerun the extractor, then
`--import`.

- `tileset.png` (928×320) → 21 named pieces, listed in `magic_cliffs.json`
- `sky.png`, `sea.png`, `far-grounds.png`, `clouds.png` → parallax layers,
  copied through byte for byte

`archway` has two jobs. It is set dressing in the levels that place it — the
pack hangs it in the sky, and so does this game — and it is also the end-of-level
marker, drawn lit from behind by
[portal.gd](../../portal.gd) so the hole through it reads as a way out. The
sprite is used unmodified for both; only the light behind it differs, and
portal.gd reads the hole's shape off the sprite's own alpha so a recut piece
would still be lit through the gap and not across the rock.

The pack's own enemies (`Assets/Enemies Files/`) are **not used**. Their
characters are drawn ~24–32 px tall against 16 px tiles; this game's cast is 73
and 65 px, so they are not to the same scale, and the project already has a
fighting enemy.

## Geometry

Tiles are **16×16**. Nothing is resampled and nothing needs to be: the cast is
drawn at 0.75 world size against the game's 4/3 canvas magnification, which nets
to exactly 1, so a pack pixel lands on one screen pixel. This is crisp
14-colour pixel art and a resize would destroy it — see the note on `SCALE` and
`TEXTURE_SCALE` in `scripts/extract_anti_davis.py`, and the warning against
resampling Ansimuz's other pack in its own provenance.

One 16 px tile is therefore **12 world units**. The player stands 73 px, so he
is about 4.6 tiles tall; his full-speed jump clears 213 world units, or about 18
tiles, which is why the islands here are spaced far more generously than the
pack's own preview suggests.

### The grass line

Every ground piece in the sheet is drawn against one horizontal line — the grass
surface, the row a character's feet rest on. It sits at **sheet y = 190**, so a
piece cut at sheet y has its surface `190 − y` rows below its own top edge. That
is what lets the 111 px left cap, with its tall sparse tufts, meet the 22 px
strip with short dense ones without a step at the seam.

The extractor re-checks this on every run against the grass *colour* band rather
than the alpha channel: the tufts above the surface are dense enough to read as
solid, so no opacity threshold can tell them from the ground. A tolerance of two
rows is allowed, because a cap's grass rolls over its shoulder a row later than a
flat strip's, and a genuinely re-laid-out sheet would be wrong by far more.

`surface` is only meaningful for pieces you stand on — marked `standable` in the
manifest. Set dressing is anchored by its bottom edge instead.
