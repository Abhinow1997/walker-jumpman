## Why this matters here

Every other sheet in this project is ripped commercial art — the Anti-Davis
player is a Little Fighter 2 fan mod, the Bandit and the items are LF2 rips, and
`mech-healthbar.png` has no known origin at all. That is fine for coursework and
would be a takedown on itch.io or Steam, both of which require you to warrant
you hold the rights.

This pack is the first art in the game that carries no such problem. Terrain,
background and set dressing for The Fractured Isles are all CC0.

## What is used

Generated by `scripts/extract_magic_cliffs.py` from `Assets/Environment/PNG/`.
**Never hand-edit anything in this folder** — rerun the extractor, then
`--import`.

- `tileset.png` (928×320) → 21 named pieces, listed in `magic_cliffs.json`
- `sky.png`, `sea.png`, `far-grounds.png`, `clouds.png` → parallax layers,
  copied through byte for byte

The pack's own enemies (`Assets/Enemies Files/`) are **not used**. Their
characters are drawn ~24–32 px tall against 16 px tiles; this game's cast is 73
and 65 px, so they are not to the same scale, and the project already has a
fighting enemy.

## Geometry

Tiles are **16×16**. Nothing is resampled and nothing needs to be: the cast is
drawn at 0.75 world size against the game's 4/3 canvas magnification, which nets
to exactly 1, so a pack pixel lands on one screen pixel. This is crisp
14-colour pixel art and a resize would destroy it — see the note on `SCALE` and
`TEXTURE_SCALE` in `scripts/extract_anti_davis.py`, and the warning against
resampling Ansimuz's other pack in its own provenance.

One 16 px tile is therefore **12 world units**. The player stands 73 px, so he
is about 4.6 tiles tall; his full-speed jump clears 213 world units, or about 18
tiles, which is why the islands here are spaced far more generously than the
pack's own preview suggests.

### The grass line

Every ground piece in the sheet is drawn against one horizontal line — the grass
surface, the row a character's feet rest on. It sits at **sheet y = 190**, so a
piece cut at sheet y has its surface `190 − y` rows below its own top edge. That
is what lets the 111 px left cap, with its tall sparse tufts, meet the 22 px
strip with short dense ones without a step at the seam.

The extractor re-checks this on every run against the grass *colour* band rather
than the alpha channel: the tufts above the surface are dense enough to read as
solid, so no opacity threshold can tell them from the ground. A tolerance of two
rows is allowed, because a cap's grass rolls over its shoulder a row later than a
flat strip's, and a genuinely re-laid-out sheet would be wrong by far more.

`surface` is only meaningful for pieces you stand on — marked `standable` in the
manifest. Set dressing is anchored by its bottom edge instead.
